answer:
  - "Add up every instalment plus any fee, then compare that total with the price for paying in full today. If the two are the same and there are no fees, the plan can be genuinely free. If the plan costs more, the difference is what you pay for spreading it out. (EMI means equated monthly instalment: the same payment each month.)"
  - "A 0% plan is still credit, even when it costs nothing extra. Fees, a lost discount for paying now, or charges after a late or incomplete payment can make it cost more, so the terms matter as much as the price."

steps:
  - action: "Find the price for paying in full today."
    why: "Some shops charge the same either way; others give a discount for paying now, or set a higher price for instalments. Asking at the checkout is fine."
  - action: "Add up the plan: every instalment, plus any processing fee, tax on interest, insurance or account fee."
    why: "This is what the plan costs you if every payment is on time."
    example: "12 × €100 = €1,200, with no fees."
  - action: "Subtract the price for paying now."
    why: "If the result is zero, the plan doesn't cost more. If it's more than zero, that is the cost of spreading the payments."
    example: "€1,200 − €1,140 = €60. With twelve €100 payments at the end of each month and no fees, that works like borrowing €1,140 at about 10% a year."
  - action: "Read what happens if a payment is late or fails."
    why: "A late fee, interest charged back to the day of purchase, or the plan turning into one that charges interest are the common ones."
    caution: "With deferred interest, any balance left at the deadline can bring all the interest since the day of purchase. In the lesson's simplified example, twelve €90 payments on €1,200 leave €120, and interest at 25% a year on each month's balance adds about €176. Real terms can calculate it differently."
