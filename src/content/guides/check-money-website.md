  - "Reach the site in a way you found yourself: the official app, a bookmark you saved, or an address printed on your card or statement. Then read the web address carefully: fake sites often add a word, swap a letter or use a different ending."
  - "A padlock in the browser only means the connection is private, not that the company is real. Passing these checks lowers the risk; it doesn't remove it."

taskTime: "a few minutes"
form: steps
steps:
  - action: "Go to the site through a route you found yourself."
    why: "The official app, a bookmark you saved earlier, or an address you type from your card, statement or contract. A link in a message, an ad or a search result can lead to a copy."
    caution: "Ads at the top of search results can point to fake sites that look like the real one."
  - action: "Compare the whole web address with the one you found yourself."
    why: "Read the domain name in the browser's address bar. It can have several parts, with endings such as .co.uk. Compare the whole name with the one you found yourself: a familiar name somewhere inside it isn't enough. If you see an \"@\" before the first \"/\", the site's name comes after it. If you can't tell which part is the name, the official app is a safer way in."
    example: "In https://yourbank.example.account-check.example/login, yourbank.example is only the front part of a longer name. The site is not yourbank.example."
  - action: "Look for small changes in the name."
    why: "An extra word (\"-secure\", \"-login\"), a swapped letter (\"rn\" for \"m\", \"0\" for \"o\") or a different ending can make a copy look right at a glance."
  - action: "Check the company somewhere else."
    why: "Your country's financial regulator may keep a public register of firms allowed to offer banking, loans or investments, and a warning list. Reach the regulator's site independently and compare the firm's web address and contact details with its entry. A matching name or registration number alone isn't enough: fake sites copy real firms' details. And a match doesn't prove an offer is safe."
  - action: "Stop before typing anything if something doesn't match."
    why: "A sign-in, a card number or a one-time code typed into a copy goes straight to whoever made it. You can always go back later through the official app."
