answer:
  - "Multiply today's cost by 1 plus the yearly inflation rate, once for each year. At 2.5% a year, €100 of shopping costs about €113 after 5 years and about €128 after 10."
  - "Turned around, after 10 years at that rate, €100 kept without earning interest buys about €78 of today's shopping. Real inflation differs by country, by year and by what you buy, so this is an illustration, not a forecast."

steps:
  - action: "Pick a cost you know: a month's shopping, rent, or a yearly bill."
    why: "A cost you pay yourself makes the result easier to picture than an index."
  - action: "Pick a yearly inflation rate."
    why: "Your country's statistics office publishes recent inflation. A range is useful: the rate of the last few years, and a lower and a higher one."
    caution: "Your own prices can rise faster or slower than the average. Rent, food and energy often move differently from each other."
  - action: "Multiply by (1 + the rate) once for each year."
    why: "Each year's rise is on top of the last, so the cost grows a little faster every year."
    example: "€100 × 1.025 × 1.025 … ten times = €100 × 1.28 ≈ €128."
  - action: "Divide instead to see what a sum then would buy in today's prices."
    why: "€100 ÷ 1.28 ≈ €78. This is what money loses over those years if it earns nothing."
