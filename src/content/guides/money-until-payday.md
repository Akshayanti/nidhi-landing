group: month
answer:
  - "Take what's in your account today. Subtract every bill and payment due before your next pay, then a rough figure for everyday spending such as food and transport."
  - "Do it in date order. A negative running balance flags a projected gap. If you set aside everyday spending a week at a time, the gap can show up earlier than the money would actually run out."
scope:
  can: "Estimate whether your money covers the days until your next pay, and flag a possible shortfall."
  cannot: "Make the money stretch, or tell you which bill to move or which cost to cut."
needs:
  - "Your banking app or your latest statement"
  - "The date of your next pay"
  - "A pen and paper, or the printable sheet below"
taskTime: "about 15 minutes"
form: steps
steps:
  - action: "Write down today's balance and the date of your next pay."
    why: "Everything else is measured against these two things. Use your own money available today, not an unused overdraft or credit, and count pending payments once. If bills leave from different accounts, keep a separate running balance for each."
  - action: "List every bill and payment due before payday, with its date."
    why: "Dates matter as much as amounts. A bill that leaves before your pay arrives can push the balance below zero even when the month adds up overall."
    example: "Rent, phone, energy, loan or card payments, subscriptions. Your app's list of scheduled payments or direct debits shows most of them."
    caution: "Bills that come once a year or once a quarter, such as insurance or a subscription renewal, are the easiest to miss. Check last year's statement for this month."
  - action: "Estimate everyday spending until payday."
    why: "Food, transport and small purchases don't arrive as bills, but they add up. A rough daily figure is enough here: add up one recent week of them on your statement and divide by 7."
    example: "€154 over 7 days is about €22 a day. With 12 days to payday, that's about €264. For everyday essentials, think of food, local transport, medicine and similar small purchases. Bills such as housing, phone and internet, energy and water, childcare and minimum debt payments belong in step 2 if they fall due before payday."
  - action: "Subtract each item in date order and write the balance after each one, without counting anything twice."
    why: "Set aside everyday spending at the start of each week, or subtract it day by day, so the balance never looks better than it is. Weekly amounts flag a gap early; day-by-day entries around a gap show more closely when money might actually run short."
  - action: "Read the last line, and the lowest one."
    why: "The last line estimates what's left on payday. The lowest line shows the largest projected gap, if there is one. With weekly amounts, its date may come earlier than the real shortfall."
    caution: "This is an estimate. A daily figure is an average, and some weeks cost more than others, so a small margin can disappear."
