description: "Compare the amount that arrives, for the same amount sent. A transfer costs a fee plus an exchange-rate margin, so a quote with no fee can still deliver less."
outcome: "You'll know which quote delivers more today, and what each costs in fee and rate together."
group: borders
answer:
  - "Compare the amount that actually arrives, in the other currency, for the same amount sent."
  - "Transfer costs can include a fee and a margin in the exchange rate, which is often not shown as a cost. A quote with no transfer fee can still deliver less if its rate is worse."
scope:
  can: "Compare two or more transfer quotes on the same basis."
  cannot: "Tell you whether a provider is licensed where you live, or predict tomorrow's exchange rate."
needs:
  - "Two quotes for the same amount, from providers' websites or apps"
taskTime: "about 10 minutes"
form: example
stepsHeading: "Try it with your own quotes"
steps:
  - action: "Get both quotes close together in time, for the same total amount leaving your account, the same delivery speed and the same way of paying."
    why: "Rates move during the day, and faster delivery or paying by card can cost more. Comparing like with like is the whole point."
  - action: "Find every fee, and how it's charged."
    why: "Some providers add the fee on top, others take it out of the amount before converting. Both change what arrives."
    caution: "The receiving bank may charge its own fee, which neither quote shows. The person receiving the money can ask their bank."
  - action: "Find the exchange rate each provider uses."
    why: "It's usually shown as \"1 EUR = 89.10 INR\" or similar. When both rates show rupees per euro, a lower rate means fewer rupees for each euro."
  - action: "Compare the \"recipient gets\" amounts."
    why: "Compare the amounts expected to arrive after all known deductions, for the same total amount paid. Fees the quotes don't show, from a receiving or intermediary bank, can still change the result."
  - action: "If you want, compare each rate with the mid-market rate."
    why: "The mid-market rate is the midpoint between the prices at which banks buy and sell a currency, the one news sites and search engines show. The gap between it and a provider's rate is a cost you pay, even when there's no fee."
    example: "In the example above, Quote A costs 2.0% of the money's value at the mid-market rate and Quote B 2.7%, though only Quote A has a fee."
