answer:
  - "Net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. Add up your accounts, savings, pension and anything you could sell, at today's value. Add up every debt at today's balance. Subtract the second total from the first."
  - "The result can be negative, especially with a student loan or a new mortgage. That is common, and it is where the count starts, not a judgement."

form: steps
steps:
  - action: "List what you own, at today's value."
    why: "Current and savings accounts, cash, investments, a home, pensions that show a pot value, and things you could sell, such as a car. Include only your share of anything owned jointly. Today's value is what it would fetch now, not what you paid."
    example: "A car bought for €12,000 a few years ago might sell for €6,000 today. €6,000 goes on the list."
  - action: "List what you owe, at today's balance."
    why: "Cards, loans, an overdraft, a mortgage, buy now pay later plans and money owed to family. Use what is left to pay, not the monthly payment."
  - action: "Add up each list, then subtract what you owe from what you own."
    why: "That is your net worth. Round numbers are fine: a figure that is roughly right is more useful than no figure."
  - action: "Write the date next to it."
    why: "The date shows when the balances and values were recorded. Some people repeat the count every few months or once a year to compare."
    caution: "A home or a pension can be hard to value, and may not be money you can spend. Some pensions promise an income later instead of showing a pot value; note those separately as not valued here. Net worth is not the cash you have available today."
