answer:
  - "Ask each lender for the same amount over the same length of time. Then add up everything you would pay: every monthly payment plus any fees. The offer with the lower total costs less to borrow."
  - "A lower interest rate or a lower monthly payment doesn't always mean a cheaper loan: a fee can outweigh a rate that is a little lower. APR means annual percentage rate. It expresses the interest and the charges that local rules include as one yearly rate; which charges, and how it is calculated, differ by country."

steps:
  - action: "Ask both lenders for offers that give you the same amount to use, over the same length of loan."
    why: "A fee taken out of the loan can leave you with less than the headline amount. A longer loan has lower monthly payments but usually costs more in total, so offers over different lengths can't be compared fairly."
  - action: "Write down each offer's monthly payment, number of payments, fees and APR."
    why: "Fees can be called arrangement, processing, origination or administration fees. Ask whether any insurance or account is required with the loan, and what it costs."
  - action: "Add up every payment and every charge you must pay that isn't already in those payments."
    why: "For equal monthly payments, multiply the payment by the number of payments. Add a different first or last payment, required insurance or account charges, and a fee paid at the start. Count each fee once: a fee added to the loan is already in the payments. The total minus the amount you receive is what the loan costs."
    example: "Offer B: €239.00 × 48 = €11,472, plus the €400 fee = €11,872."
  - action: "Check what the numbers leave out."
    why: "Is the rate fixed for the whole loan? Is there a charge for paying early? What happens after a late payment? These can matter as much as the total."
    caution: "If the rate can change, the total is only an estimate. Asking what the payment would be if the rate rose by 2 points shows how much it could move."
