steps:
  - action: "Write down the bills that are the same every month."
    why: "Rent or mortgage, insurance, phone and internet, and the minimum payment on each other debt. Count each payment once, even if it fits more than one heading. Your statements and contracts show the amounts."
  - action: "Look back two or three months for costs that change: food, energy, transport."
    why: "One month can be unusual. An average of a few months gives a starting figure. If heating, transport or other costs change with the seasons, older statements or yearly bills show what those months miss."
    example: "Food was €290, €340 and €330 over three months: about €320 a month."
  - action: "Add the bills that come once or twice a year, divided by 12."
    why: "Insurance paid yearly, a licence, a membership you can't drop. They're easy to forget because they don't appear every month."
  - action: "Decide what counts as essential for you, then add it up."
    why: "A common definition is what your household needs to keep going: housing, food, energy and water, necessary transport, insurance and required debt payments. Medicine, childcare, disability-related costs or support for family members may belong here too. What counts depends on your circumstances."
    caution: "For debts, use the minimum payment the agreement currently requires. Keep any extra repayments out of this figure."
