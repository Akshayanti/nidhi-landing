**Amount display when space is short (issue #667, 2026-11-17).** An amount is one token: sign, currency symbol, digits, decimal part, percent sign and any short suffix (M, Cr, lakh) never separate. Harm ranking, worst first: sign split from figure (reads as the opposite sign, flips which option is ahead); sideways scroll hiding the tail (silent 1000x misreading); a break inside a group; a break at a group separator (worst in cs, where "432 Kč" on the next line looks complete, in es where "98.765." looks like a sentence end, and in Indian grouping, where "65,432" looks like a whole Western number); smaller type; shortening.
**Why:** a new reader takes the first line as the whole amount, and a sign error changes the conclusion, not only the size.
**How to apply:** preferred order is whole on its own line, then shortened (only where the figure is already approximate or the exact figure is visible elsewhere on the page or export; every amount in one comparison shortened together; two different values must never shorten to the same text), then smaller type, never a break inside, never sideways scroll. Monte Carlo and invest or pay off already round in words and say the spreadsheet has every digit; the loan comparison is exact and shortening there can collapse two offers to one figure. Breaking a range at "to" is fine. U+2212 does not by itself stop a break before a following currency symbol (observed: "−" / "€1,520" at 100%).

**Canadian APR is not "monthly rate x 12" (verified 2026-11-20, #699).** Federal Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations SOR/2021-181 s.47(1): APR = C/(T x P) x 100 (total cost of borrowing / (term in years x average principal outstanding)); s.32: APR = annual interest rate when interest is the only cost. Provinces (e.g. Ontario O. Reg. 17/05) use the same harmonised average-principal formula. Not compounded, but not the US actuarial IRR x 12 either; fees enter undiscounted. Interest Act s.6: mortgage rates stated "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance" (semi-annual is convention, not mandated). US Reg Z (periodic rate x periods) is fine. India/Australia/Japan in the loan FAQ list were not verified.
**Refinance break-even (math.ts refinanceComparison, read 2026-11-20).** Counted from the refinance month; cash cost = closing costs not rolled in + old loan's early repayment penalty; undiscounted; uses payments due, not extras. Rolled-in fee switches to a balance-adjusted mode. In cash mode a longer new term can reach break-even and still cost more in total: the hint should point to the totals.
**Balance-adjusted break-even (rolled-in fee), derived 2026-11-20.** Position(k) = -(rolled fee + penalty) + cumulative(old interest - new interest), because paid + owed moves by each month's interest. So it can cross zero and then fall again once the current loan would have ended (new loan still charging interest): a longer new term can break even here and still cost more in total. The "totals above show which" caveat applies to this mode too.
**Wording of the Canada rule:** Interest Act s.6 requires a blended-payment mortgage rate to be stated "calculated yearly or half-yearly, not in advance". Say "half-yearly is usual; the law allows yearly or half-yearly", not "not a legal requirement" (reads as no rule at all). Canada's APR formula (C/(T x P)) can differ from the IRR x 12 figure by a few tenths of a point on a short loan with a large fee (1,000 over 12 months, 0% interest, fee 50: about 9.6% by IRR x 12, 9.2% by C/(T x P)), so avoid "slightly".

**Inflation "past" mode, World Bank + BIS monthly join (reviewed 2026-11-25, proposal only).** Chain avg(Y)/avg(F) x P(M)/mean12(Y) = P(M)/avg(F): algebraically exact if both are the same CPI (scale-free). Verdict SOUND WITH CONDITIONS: tolerance 0.2 pt (or relative for high inflation), link year Y must agree, India/UK/Peru/Argentina fall back to year-only (UK gap looks like WB using CPIH vs BIS CPI, unverified); total-rise line must reach month M so it matches the result; "since the 2025 average", not "since 2025"; full-year average only; NSA month carries seasonality; salary caveat: start is a year average, can be off by about half a year's inflation.

**Loan fixed-then-variable and lump-sum validation (#818, #796, reviewed 2026-11-26).** Blank later rate or initial period refused, 0% later rate accepted (a typed number is not blank). A change at or after the term end (or a lump sum after payoff) gives correct totals for the inputs: the fix is a note saying the box was not used, never an error (extras can move payoff legitimately). Initial period >= 1,200 is refused with "must be a positive number", which is wrong wording, and it is inconsistent with 400 on a 360 loan being accepted. Fractional initial periods are still rounded silently (0.5 -> 1), unlike lump-sum months. The example offer (250,000, 6.5%, 360 m) totals 568,861.58 fixed; after 60 m at 9% it totals 683,995.09 (hand check: B60 about 234,028, recast about 1,963.95 x 300). "Common: 60, 84 or 120 months" is the US ARM convention (5/1, 7/1, 10/1).

**Zero-total tolerance and "0%" wording (#824, #888, reviewed 2026-11-27).** Net worth: |total| <= 2(k+1) eps x gross-before-cancelling, k = currencies. k+1 bounds only conversion and cross-currency adds; row sums inside a currency carry up to (rows) eps x gross, so it is not a rigorous bound. Residue shown only when the true total is exactly zero at huge gross; a real tiny total gives absurd shares too, so filing was fine. Rigorous fix: sum rows per currency in integer minor units (exact to about 9e13 major units), then k+1 holds. Rule for every tool: a figure written as 0% must not sit beside a direction word ("rose", "would lose buying power"); the flat test must use the same rounding as the display (shownNumber), which gives a true strict "less than half the last digit".

**Small amounts and no-rate shares (#868 pt 3, #887, #888 follow-up, reviewed 2026-11-27).** Inflation "about": two significant figures from 0.01 to 100, three above, so the scale is coherent; "less than 0.01" floor fine; "¥1.3" fine because the figure is a buying-power measure, not a payable sum. Sheet cells that hold the page's two-figure number make the note "exact figures" untrue for them (reword later, non-blocking). Net worth `sharesUnknown`: must also exclude a redacted share link whose net worth was exactly zero (all shares 0 with noShares set), or it says "Rate unavailable" where rates existed. "Rate unavailable" on the spending currency's own row is loose (its rate is 1; the share is what is unknown); "Share unknown without exchange rates" would be exact, non-blocking. Real-return note by the figure as shown to one decimal: correct.

**Loan notes and inflation sheet note (#914, #915, #922, reviewed 2026-11-27).** "Rate does not change within this term" only when initial period >= term; paid off early before the change already has its own card line, and there the later rate still shapes the contract APR, so "not used" would be false: keep them apart. A lump sum in the payoff month is unused when that month's regular payment cleared the loan (applied after the payment); a lump larger than the balance counts only what was applied, no note. Totals everywhere come from the schedule, so unused sums are excluded. Inflation sheet: a "general" cell appears when the page's two-figure figure has more decimals than the currency (¥1.3, €0.074), not only below the unit, so "too small to show at that unit" is untrue; say "shown with more decimals than the currency has... as the page shows it, to two significant figures".
