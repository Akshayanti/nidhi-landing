# Setting Financial Goals: From Vague Wishes to Concrete Targets

## Caption

"Buy a house someday." "Retire comfortably." "Be debt-free." Almost none of these are goals as stated. They are wishes: directions without a destination, ambitions without a finish line.

The difference between a wish and a goal is a number and a date. "Buy a house someday" is a wish. "€45,000 deposit by June 2031" is a goal. A goal can be measured, tracked, adjusted, and eventually hit. A wish can only be hoped for.

A real goal needs four parts: a target amount, a date, a path (monthly contribution at a realistic return), and a container (cash, mixed, or investments) that matches the timeline.

The cost of delaying long-term goals is non-linear. A €500,000 retirement target at 65, assuming 4% real return (the conservative European baseline), costs ~€420 a month if you start at 25. The same target costs ~€1,360 a month if you start at 45.

## Slide 2 (list)

title: A real goal has four parts. A wish has none of them.

1. :coins: A specific target amount, in a currency of choice
2. :calendar: A specific date, month and year
3. :trendUp: A path: monthly contribution at a realistic return
4. :wallet: A container: cash, mixed, or investments, matching the timeline

Missing any one and the goal quietly slides back into a wish.

---

## Slide 4 (list)

title: Three time horizons, three containers

1. :clock: Short term, 0 to 3 years: cash. Stability over growth.
2. :scale: Medium term, 3 to 7 years: blend. Some cash, some conservative investments.
3. :trendUp: Long term, 7 plus years: diversified investments. Volatility averages out.

Mismatching bucket and container is the most common goal-setting error.

## Slide 5 (stat)

label: €500,000 retirement target at 65, 4% real return assumed
hero: Start at 25 → ~€420/mo. || Start at 45 → ~€1,360/mo.
caption: Same target. ~3× the monthly cost.
note: At 25: ~€420 a month for 40 years. At 35: ~€720 a month for 30 years. At 45: ~€1,360 a month for 20 years. At 55: ~€3,400 a month for 10 years. The late starter contributes more than €200,000 extra and ends up with the same number.

source: Assumes 4% real return, the conservative European baseline. Figures rounded.

## Slide 6 (list)

title: When goals compete, math sets the order

1. Starter buffer in place
2. High interest debt under control
3. Full buffer rebuilt
4. Employer match captured
5. Long term retirement contributions
6. Medium then short term goals

Priority follows interest rates and timelines, not whichever goal feels loudest.

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: Goals drift. Review annually, not weekly.

Markets move. Life changes. Assumptions need updating.

Once a year is enough. Quarterly works if you actually act on it. Weekly checks create anxiety, not progress.

Written goals survive better than mental ones. One page is plenty.
