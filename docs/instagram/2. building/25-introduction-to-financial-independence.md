# Introduction to Financial Independence: What It Means and Why It Matters

## Caption

Most people think of retirement as a date. An age the calendar picks. Financial independence is a different idea: it's a number. The point where investments cover expenses indefinitely, and work becomes optional.

Two terms get used almost interchangeably, and shouldn't be. **Financial independence (FI)** is the target. Assets large enough that work is optional. **FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early)** is the same target reached early enough to actually retire on it. Same math, different finish line.

The simplest formula: FI number = annual expenses divided by safe withdrawal rate. The US-derived "4% rule" gives 25× annual spending. European markets have historically had higher inflation and lower long-run real returns, so 3 to 3.5% is the more conservative anchor. We use 3% throughout, which is 33× annual expenses. €30,000 a year to spend means roughly €1,000,000 in invested assets. The number is set entirely by what you spend, not what you earn.

Four flavors cover most goals. Lean FIRE (minimal lifestyle, fastest path). Traditional FIRE (current lifestyle, indefinitely). Fat FIRE (current lifestyle plus margin). Coast FIRE (stop saving, let compounding finish the job).

The lever that actually matters: savings rate. Two people earning very different salaries at the same savings rate reach FI in roughly the same number of years.

## Slide 4 (prose)

title: The crossover point: when work becomes optional

The crossover is the month investment income first exceeds expenses.

Before it: work income keeps your life going. After it: investments do, even if you keep working.

Tracking it turns financial independence from an abstract distant target into a moving gap that closes month by month.

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: FIRE is not only for extreme savers

20% saved consistently lands at FI in your late sixties, close to traditional retirement age. That is the path most state retirement systems implicitly assume.

30 to 35% saved cuts roughly 10 to 15 years off that.

Coast FIRE can arrive in your thirties: enough invested that compounding alone finishes the job by 65, even if you stop adding.

---

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: Why we use 3%, not 4%

The famous "4% rule" comes from US data over a 30 year window. US inflation, US equity returns, US bond yields.

European markets have historically had higher inflation and lower long-run real returns. The conservative European calibration is 3 to 3.5%, moving the target to 28 to 33 times annual expenses.

We use 3% throughout these examples, which is 33×. We'll come back to the edge cases in a later post.
