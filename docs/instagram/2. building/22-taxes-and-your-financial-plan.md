# Taxes and Your Financial Plan: How Taxation Affects Every Decision

## Caption

You earn one number. You see another. You spend a third. Each gap is tax, and most plans only think about the first one.

Taxes hit your plan in four distinct places. Income tax (gross to take-home). Tax on dividends, interest, and rental income (the year you receive them). Capital gains (when you sell a winner). Retirement income tax (on withdrawals from pre-tax accounts).

The compounding cost is easy to underestimate. A 7% annual return taxed at a 25% effective drag becomes 5.25%. Over 40 years, the same starting amount ends roughly half the size.

The fix is structural, not heroic. Plan in after-tax terms. Use tax-advantaged accounts where they fit. Don't trade frequently in taxable accounts. Look up your specific country's rules before any large move.

## Slide 4 (comparison)

title: Two patterns of tax-advantaged account

**Pre-tax in, taxed on withdrawal**

Contribute before income tax. Money grows untaxed. Pay income tax when you withdraw.

Often suits people in a high bracket now, expecting a lower bracket in retirement.

===

**Post-tax in, tax-free withdrawal**

Contribute already-taxed money. Money grows untaxed. Withdraw without further tax on the growth.

Often suits younger savers, low-bracket years, or anyone expecting future tax rates to rise.

---

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: Capital gains are realized only when you sell

Holding an investment that has risen in value triggers no tax. Selling it does.

This is why frequent trading in a taxable account is expensive. Every sale at a profit may be taxed, and the compounding you would otherwise get on the untaxed amount is lost.

Buy and hold isn't only a strategy. It is tax efficiency by default.

---

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: Net is the only number you can actually spend

Expenses live in after-tax money. Savings rate is best computed on after-tax income. Retirement targets are after-tax portfolios that fund after-tax expenses.

A 7% return is not what you keep. The number after dividends, interest, and capital gains tax is.

Plans built on gross numbers are not pessimistic enough.

## Slide 7 (list)

title: Mistakes that quietly cost the most

1. Planning in gross instead of net
2. Trading frequently in a taxable account
3. Skipping the employer match
4. Leaving tax-advantaged contribution room unused
5. Assuming retirement income is automatically tax-free
