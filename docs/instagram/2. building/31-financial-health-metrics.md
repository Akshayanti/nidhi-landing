# Financial Health Metrics: How to Know If You're on Track

## Caption

Your net worth is €150,000. Is that good? Impossible to say without context. A 25 year old with €150,000 net worth and no liabilities is in a radically different place than a 55 year old with €150,000 net worth and €400,000 of mortgage left. Same number. Completely different situations.

Net worth is a snapshot. Ratios are the diagnosis. Six metrics, read together, tell you whether your finances are healthy, where they're drifting, and what to adjust while the fix is still small.

Debt-to-asset ratio (how leveraged you are). Liquid asset percentage (how flexible). Emergency fund coverage (how resilient). Savings rate (how fast you're building). Debt-to-income ratio (how strained cash flow is). Income replacement ratio (how close you are to financial independence).

The goal isn't a perfect score on each. It's catching the ones drifting into unhealthy territory early, while the fixes are still small.

## Slide 3 (prose)

title: Healthy zones, not optimal targets

Most ratios don't have a single right answer. They have ranges.

A young homeowner with a 90% loan-to-value mortgage starts with a high debt-to-asset ratio. That's expected, not unhealthy.

The point isn't optimising every score. It is catching drift toward the unhealthy zone early, while the fix is still small.

source: Bands are directional rules of thumb. Real outcomes still depend on returns and time.

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: Direction matters more than level

A 60% debt-to-asset ratio is high. Going from 40% to 60% over two years is more useful information than the level itself.

A 20% savings rate is healthy. Sliding from 30% to 20% over a year is the early signal of lifestyle creep.

Trends surface drift before crises do.

---

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: The combination tells the real story

Healthy savings rate but low liquid percentage: probably locked into illiquid assets. Flexibility could bite you.

Low debt-to-income but low savings rate: paying for lifestyle from income, not building wealth.

Strong metrics but low income replacement: set up well, just early in the journey.

Each metric is informative. The pattern across all six is decisive.

## Slide 7 (list)

title: How people misuse the metrics

1. Chasing a single ideal number instead of zones
2. Reading current level without reading direction
3. Measuring once, reacting forever
4. Comparing to others without context
5. Using the dashboard for guilt instead of decisions
