# Managing Money Across Currencies: When Your Finances Cross Borders

## Caption

You live in one country, earn in one currency, spend in another, send money home in a third, and might retire in a fourth. On paper, one net worth. In reality, multiple currencies pulling in different directions. This is the normal shape of expat finances, not an edge case.

Three currencies decide most of the picture. The currency you earn in. The one you spend in. The one your major future plans will live in. When all three match, currency is not a concern. When any two diverge, exchange rates become part of your plan.

Currency concentration is the diversification dimension most people miss. Earning, saving, investing, and retiring all in one currency is fine if your future life is genuinely single-currency. It's hidden risk if your retirement, family, or relocation crosses any currency border.

You don't need to trade FX (foreign exchange). You need to map your flows once a year, match assets roughly to obligations, and minimise transfer friction.

## Slide 3 (prose)

title: Net worth moves even when nothing else does

Hold a £50,000 pension. The pound drops 15% against the euro. Your net worth in euros drops 15%, with no change to the pension itself.

No headline. No transaction. Just exchange rate noise.

Over decades, these moves average out. Over the years right before you need the money, they don't.

## Slide 4 (prose)

title: This is under-diversification hiding in plain sight

Earn in euros. Spend in euros. Save in euros. Invest in a euro fund. Retire in euros.

That is full euro concentration. Fine if your future life really is fully in euros.

Hidden risk if your future plans, family, or relocation cross any currency border.

source: Currency risk here means exposure spread across your real life, not day-trading FX.

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: Match assets to obligations. Don't trade currencies.

The case for matching: predictable. The case for actively trading currencies: weak. Most retail FX trading underperforms after spreads and fees.

Pick low-cost transfer providers. Batch transfers. Avoid frequent moves.

Annual review is enough. Currency is daily noise and yearly signal.
