# Your Financial Dashboard: What to Track and How Often

## Caption

There are two common failure modes in personal finance tracking. Ignoring everything until tax season or a crisis forces a look. Or checking every app every day and reacting to every small move.

A dashboard sits between both: a small set of metrics on a known cadence, designed to surface drift early without creating noise.

Four layers, four cadences. Monthly: net worth, cash flow, savings rate, debt balances (15 minutes). Quarterly: asset allocation, currency mix, goal progress, emergency fund coverage. Annually: full financial health review, goal calibration, projection vs actual. Plus event-driven reviews when life changes meaningfully.

The point is signals, not constant measurement. Most of your net worth, most of the time, doesn't move because of anything you did this week. It moves on the timescales of months and years.

## Slide 2 (prose)

title: Two failure modes, one fix

One: ignore everything until tax season or a crisis forces a look.

Two: check everything every day, react to every small move, treat tracking as a substitute for deciding.

A dashboard sits between both. Small set. Known cadence. Early signal. Otherwise, out of the way.

---

## Slide 3 (list)

title: Four layers, four cadences

1. Monthly: net worth, cash flow, savings rate, debt balances
2. Quarterly: asset allocation, currency mix, goal progress, emergency fund coverage
3. Annually: full health metrics, goal calibration, projection vs actual
4. Event-driven: only on major life or income changes

---

## Slide 4 (list)

title: Monthly: 4 numbers, 15 minutes

1. Net worth: assets minus liabilities
2. Monthly cash flow: income minus expenses for the month
3. Savings rate: as a 3 month rolling average
4. Debt balances: declining at expected pace

If you do only this, monthly, you have 80% of the dashboard's value.

---

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: Quarterly: zoom out for drift

Asset allocation: drifted from target by more than 5%? Time to consider rebalancing.

Currency mix: gap growing between what you hold and what you'll eventually spend?

Goal progress: on track, behind, or ahead against your written targets?

Emergency fund coverage: still 3 to 6 months as expenses creep upward?

---

## Slide 6 (list)

title: What not to track

1. Daily portfolio values: noise, not signal
2. Individual stock prices, if you hold funds
3. Most market news: entertainment, not action
4. Friends' finances: comparison, not clarity

Removing these isn't neglect. It's focus.

---

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: A simple sheet beats any system you don't update

Date. Total assets. Total liabilities. Net worth. Income. Expenses. Savings rate. Notes.

Eight columns is enough.

The notes column is the one most people skip and most need: why net worth dipped, what was a one-off, what's worth watching. Past you explaining to future you.
