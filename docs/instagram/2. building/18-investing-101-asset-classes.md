# Investing 101: Asset Classes and How They Work

## Caption

You've heard of stocks since you were a teenager. Could you explain what each asset class actually does in a portfolio?

Most people invest in whatever their bank suggests, without knowing the building blocks. There are four, and each plays a very different role.

Stocks. You own a small piece of a company. Highest historical return, biggest short term swings. Long horizon money.

Bonds. You lend to a government or company. Smaller return, but the counterweight. Often rises when stocks fall.

Real estate. You own property. Rent and appreciation. The only major asset class where borrowing to invest is standard.

Cash. You park money you need soon. Not an investment in the growth sense. A holding place.

No single class wins in every condition. The mix is what creates the portfolio.

## Slide 2 (prose)

title: Stocks. You own a piece.

A share is a small piece of a company. If the company grows, your share grows. If it pays profits as dividends, you get cash.

Highest historical return of the four. Also the largest short term swings.

Right place for money you won't need for 5 plus years. Wrong place for next month's rent.

---

## Slide 3 (prose)

title: Bonds. You lend to someone.

A bond is a loan to a government or company. They pay you interest at fixed times and return your money on a set date.

More stable than stocks. Often rises when stocks fall. The counterweight in a portfolio.

Returns barely beat inflation. Bonds protect capital. They don't build it.

---

## Slide 4 (prose)

title: Real estate. You own property.

You earn rent from tenants and capital appreciation as values rise.

The only major asset class where borrowing to invest is standard. A mortgage amplifies both gains and losses.

Hard to sell quickly. Plan in years, not days.

---

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: Cash. You park the money.

Savings accounts, money market funds, term deposits. Tiny interest, near zero swing, instant access.

Cash is not an investment in the growth sense. It is a holding place for money you need soon.

Useful for the next month. Not the next decade.

---

## Slide 6 (comparison)

title: Different return, different role

**Builders**

Stocks: 5 to 7% real return per year
Real estate: 3 to 5% plus rental yield

Long horizon. Larger swings. Where wealth grows.

===

**Anchors**

Bonds: 2 to 4% real return per year
Cash: 0 to 2%

Shorter horizon. Smaller swings. Where wealth waits.

source: Historical broad averages, developed markets. Past performance is not a forecast.

---

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: One purchase, hundreds of holdings

A global stock index fund gives you a small piece of thousands of companies in a single buy.

A bond index fund gives you exposure to hundreds of loans.

Fees typically 0.1 to 0.3% a year. You buy the asset class itself, not individual winners.

The simplest entry point most investors actually use.
