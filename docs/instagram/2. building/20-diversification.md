# Diversification: Why You Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket

## Caption

Same employer. Same country. Same currency. Most net worth audits reveal three bets on the same story dressed up as diversification.

Diversification means owning a mix of investments that don't all respond to the same events the same way. The volatility of the whole becomes smaller than the volatility of its parts, because the parts don't all line up.

You diversify across asset class, geography, sector, company size, currency, and time. Most people only think about the first one.

The largest concentrations are usually not in the portfolio. They are the employer, the city, the sector, the home currency. Audit those first.

Diversification is often called the only free lunch in finance. It is the rare move that reduces risk without reducing expected return, when the things you hold actually behave differently from each other.

## Slide 2 (prose)

title: Diversification is mixing things that don't move together

When one falls, another may rise or stay flat. The volatility of the whole becomes smaller than the volatility of its parts.

Not because the parts stopped moving. Because their moves don't line up.

You don't need many holdings. You need holdings that aren't secretly the same thing.

---

## Slide 3 (list)

title: Six axes of diversification, not just one

1. Asset class: stocks, bonds, real estate, cash
2. Geography: multiple countries, not just home
3. Sector: tech, healthcare, energy, finance
4. Company size: large, mid, small
5. Currency: more than the one you earn in
6. Time: regular contributions over months and years

---

## Slide 4 (prose)

title: The concentrations hiding in plain sight

Your salary, your employer stock, your pension contributions: all on one company.

Your job market, your home, your social network, your currency: all in one country.

Your role, your hub, your tech stock holdings: three bets on one economic story.

Audit these first. They are larger than the portfolio.

## Slide 5 (stat)

label: Why diversification is called the only free lunch in finance
hero: Less risk, same return
caption: Combining things that don't move together
note: Markowitz, 1952. Combining imperfectly correlated investments reduces volatility without reducing expected return. Rare in economics. The mathematical foundation of modern portfolio construction won the Nobel Prize.

---

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: Diversification is dimensions, not count

Twenty stocks in one country and one sector is one bet repeated twenty times.

A single global stock fund holds thousands of companies in dozens of countries across every major sector. Many bets in one purchase.

The question to ask before adding any holding: does this cover an exposure I don't already have? If no, it is noise.

---

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: Where diversification stops working

In severe global crises, almost everything falls together. Diversification reduces risk most of the time. Not all of the time.

You cannot diversify away the market itself. If global stocks fall 30%, a diversified stock portfolio falls roughly 30% too.

Diversification handles single stock and single country risk. Not systemic risk. That is what cash and time horizon are for.
