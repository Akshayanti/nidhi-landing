# Understanding Loan Terms: How to Compare Borrowing Options

## Caption

Bank A advertises 3.2%. Bank B advertises 3.1%. Bank B is more expensive.

This is how most people lose money on loans. They compare the wrong number. The headline rate is the marketing line. APR (annual percentage rate) folds in fees, mandatory insurance, and bundled products, and is the only honest one number comparison.

Loans come in two structural flavors. Fixed rate locks the rate; payments are predictable, no exposure to future rate rises. Variable rate tracks a reference rate; cheaper today, exposed to future shocks. Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on your cash flow stability and how long you'll hold the loan.

Amortisation is where most surprises live. On a €250,000 mortgage at 3% over 25 years, year 1 of payments is €625 interest and €561 principal. Year 25 is €3 interest and €1,182 principal. Same payment every month. The split changes dramatically.

That's why prepayment in early years is disproportionately powerful. An extra €1,000 in year 1 cancels 25 years of interest on those euros. The same €1,000 in year 24 saves almost nothing.

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: Refinancing comes down to one division

Divide the new loan's fees by your monthly saving. That's how many months until it pays for itself.

Keep the loan past that point and you come out ahead. Sell or refinance again sooner and it cost you.

Watch for a restarted schedule, prepayment penalties on the old loan, and fresh fees on the new one.

---

## Slide 7 (list)

title: How to actually compare two loan offers

1. Match the amount and term across every offer
2. Get the full APR, fees and bundles included
3. Total cost: monthly payment times payments, plus upfront fees
4. Check the fine print: penalties, rate resets, insurance
5. Stress test variable rates: could you handle a 2% jump?
