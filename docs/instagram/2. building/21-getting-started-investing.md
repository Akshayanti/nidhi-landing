# Getting Started: Investment Accounts, Automation, and Your First Steps

## Caption

You read the book. Watched the video. Got excited. Six months later, nothing happened. Almost everyone who fails to start investing has the same story.

The gap isn't motivation. It is mechanics: which account, which fund, when, how much. Once those four are answered, money moves. Until then, it doesn't.

Account types come in three flavors: regular brokerage (flexible, taxable), tax-advantaged retirement (locked until later, favourable tax treatment), and employer-sponsored (often with matching, never skip the match).

The single biggest predictor of whether you invest consistently is whether the decision happens automatically. If it requires logging in every month, you eventually skip a month. Then two. Then you stop.

A standing order on payday. A recurring buy on a fixed date. Decision made once, not every month. That is the system that beats willpower.

## Slide 3 (list)

title: Which account: three to choose from

1. Regular brokerage: flexible, taxable, no contribution limit
2. Tax-advantaged retirement: locked until later, favourable tax. Specific country vehicles are covered shortly
3. Employer-sponsored: often with matching. Capturing the match first is one of the highest-leverage early moves

---

## Slide 4 (prose)

title: What to put in it: keep it simple

A broad global index fund: thousands of companies, dozens of countries, every major sector, in one buy.

Fees 0.1 to 0.3% a year. No stock picking required.

Most actively managed funds lose to their index after fees. Boring usually wins.

---

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: When: automation beats willpower

If investing requires logging in and choosing each month, you will eventually skip a month. Then two. Then it tapers off.

If it happens automatically, it just happens.

Standing order on payday. Recurring buy on a fixed date. The decision is made once, not every month.

---

## Slide 6 (prose)

title: How much: start with what's possible, not what's perfect

€50 a month done for 20 years compounds further than €500 a month started in year 18.

You can increase the amount later. The years already behind you can't be added back.

The right month to start is the month you can comfortably begin.
