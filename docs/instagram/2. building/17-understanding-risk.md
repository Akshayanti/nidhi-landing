# Understanding Risk: What It Actually Means for Your Money

## Caption

You've been told stocks are risky and savings are safe. So you make decisions that protect you from the wrong thing.

Risk in finance is not danger. It is uncertainty about outcomes. People confuse volatility (how much something swings) with permanent loss (the money is gone). They are not the same problem, and treating them as the same is the most expensive mistake in investing.

Two limits decide how much risk you can carry. Tolerance is psychological: how much fluctuation you can stomach. Capacity is financial: how much loss your life can absorb. Most people only check one and get surprised by the other.

Time is the variable that does the heavy lifting. Over 1 year, broad stock indices have lost as much as 43%. Over 20 years, the worst observed annualised return was still positive 2%. Same market, different horizon.

You don't avoid risk. You choose which kind, and how much.

## Slide 2 (comparison)

title: Risk is uncertainty. Two outcomes, very different.

**Volatility (recoverable)**

Short term price movement.

A stock fund drops 20% and recovers in two years. Painful, not lost.

===

**Permanent loss (not recoverable)**

The money is actually gone.

A bankrupt company. A scam. A forced sale at the bottom of a drawdown.

---

## Slide 3 (comparison)

title: Two different limits, one investing decision

**Risk tolerance**

Psychological. How much fluctuation you can stomach without panicking.

A 10% drop and you can't sleep: tolerance is low.

===

**Risk capacity**

Financial. How much loss your life can absorb before plans break.

Stable income, full emergency fund, two decades to wait: capacity is high.

## Slide 5 (prose)

title: Paper loss only becomes real loss when you sell

A 30% drawdown is uncomfortable. It becomes a 30% loss the moment you sell.

What forces that sale is rarely the market itself. It's needing the money for something else: rent, a job loss, a surprise bill.

So time and capacity are really one question: can you wait this out, or will life force your hand?

---

## Slide 6 (list)

title: Risk is the price of return

1. Cash and savings: 0 to 2% return, near flat
2. Government bonds: 2 to 4% return, mild swings
3. Corporate bonds: 3 to 5% return, moderate swings
4. Diversified stocks: 5 to 7% real return, large swings
5. Individual stocks: highly variable, extreme swings

You can't get stock returns at savings stability. Anyone promising that is either confused or dishonest.

source: Historical averages, broadly representative. Real returns adjust for purchasing power change. Past performance is not a forecast.

---

## Slide 7 (prose)

title: Match risk to the lower of tolerance and capacity

High tolerance plus low capacity is still low risk.

Low tolerance plus high capacity is still low risk.

Both numbers need to align. The goal isn't zero risk, it's the right amount for your timeline and your sleep.
