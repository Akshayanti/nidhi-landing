# Liabilities: What You Owe and Why the Interest Rate Matters

## Caption

Not all debt is equally dangerous. A mortgage at 3.5% and a credit card at 22% are fundamentally different obligations. The interest rate is what separates manageable debt from a financial trap.

Here's a framework for evaluating any debt, without the guilt.

## Slide 2

>> **What is a liability?**

It is money you owe, usually with interest

Every liability reduces your net worth by its full amount

---

## Slide 3

>> **Not all debt is equally dangerous**

The interest rate determines how expensive a debt really is over time

Same concept of borrowing money can have wildly different outcomes

---

## Slide 4

>> **Example 1**

Mortgage at 3.5% on €200,000:

~€100,000 in interest over 25 years

But the home may appreciate and you're building equity

---

## Slide 5

>> **Example 2**

Credit card at 22% on €3,000:

Can cost MORE than the original amount in interest alone

You pay for your purchases twice:

Once at the register, once to the bank

---

## Slide 6

>> **Forget "good" vs "bad" debt**

Ask three questions instead:

1. What is the interest rate?
2. What did the debt buy?
3. Can I afford the payments?

---

## Slide 7

>> **Low-rate debt**

- Mortgage – cheapest rate, appreciating asset, tax-advantaged
- Student loans – invests in earning potential, worth it if the career pays off

---

## Slide 8

>> **High-rate debt**

- Credit cards – high interest, compounds daily, hard to eliminate
- Consumer loans on depreciating assets – paying interest on something losing value from day one

---

## Slide 9

>> **The rule of thumb**

- Pay off high-interest debt fast
- Don't rush low-interest debt if you can invest the difference
- Always make minimum payments on everything

No investment pays 20% guaranteed. 
But your credit card charges you exactly that

---

## Slide 10

Every liability subtracts from your net worth

But how you handle them makes all the difference
