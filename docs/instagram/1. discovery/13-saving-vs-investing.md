# Saving vs. Investing: When to Do Which

## Caption

Your emergency fund should not be in the stock market. Your retirement fund should not be in a savings account.

Both are mistakes. Both come from confusing saving with investing. Here's when to do which -- and why the sequence matters.

## Slide 2

>> **Saving**

Setting money aside in a safe, accessible place

- Goal: preservation and availability
- Risk: very low
- Return: 0-3%
- Liquidity: immediate
- Best for: money you need within 1-3 years

---

## Slide 3

>> **Investing**

Putting money into assets that can grow over time

- Goal: long-term growth
- Risk: moderate to high
- Return: 7-8% historically (stocks)
- Liquidity: days to months
- Best for: money you won't need for 5+ years

---

## Slide 4

>> **When to save**

- Emergency fund (3-6 months of expenses)
- Short-term goals under 3 years
- Known upcoming expenses

If you can't afford to lose 30% of it right when you need it, save

---

## Slide 5

>> **When to invest**

- Retirement (5+ years away)
- Long-term wealth building
- Goals more than 5 years out

The longer your time horizon, the stronger the case for investing

---

## Slide 6

>> **The cost of only saving**

- €10,000 in savings at 1%, with 2.5% inflation:
- After 10 years: €11,046 in the account
- But only €8,629 in purchasing power

You gained €1,046 in interest
You lost over €1,300 in real value

---

## Slide 7

>> **The risk of investing too early**

Invest your emergency fund. Market drops 25%. You lose your job the same month

You're forced to sell at a loss

Investing money you might need soon is speculation, not planning

---

## Slide 8

>> **The right sequence**

1. Build a starter emergency fund (1 month)
2. Pay off high-interest debt (credit cards first)
3. Complete your emergency fund (3-6 months)
4. Start investing (long-term surplus)

Skip a step and you create fragility

---

## Slide 9

>> **The grey zone: 3-5 years**

Money you need in 3-5 years is the hardest to place

- Conservative: high-yield savings (certainty)
- Moderate: split between savings and bonds
- Aggressive: invest, knowing you might need to delay

It depends on how flexible your timeline is

---

## Slide 10

>> **What you can do**

1. Money for 1-3 years: save
2. Money for 5+ years: invest
3. Don't leave long-term money in savings: it loses value
4. Don't invest your safety net: that's the price of having it

---

## Slide 11

Saving and investing aren't competing strategies

They're complementary tools with different jobs

Use each one where it belongs, in the right order
