## Slide 1

**€10,000 under your mattress in 2005**
**= €6,100 of stuff today**

You lost 40%
Without spending a cent

---

## Slide 2

>> **Purchasing power**

Purchasing power is how much stuff your money can actually buy

Groceries cost €100 today, €110 next year?

Your purchasing power dropped 10%, even though your bank balance didn't change

---

## Slide 3

>> **Real vs Nominal Value**

- Nominal Value: The number on your account
- Real Value: What that number can buy

They're different
And the gap only grows over time

---

## Slide 4

>> **Inflation: The force behind it**

Prices rise ~2-3% per year on average.

A little inflation is normal

The problem is what it does over long periods

---

## Slide 5

€10,000 under your mattress

- 2005: buys €10,000 worth of goods
- 2015: buys ~€7,800 worth of goods
- 2025: buys ~€6,100 worth of goods

You lost nearly 40% of your purchasing power without spending a cent

---

## Slide 6

Even in a savings account:

€10,000 earning 0.5%, inflation at 2.5%

- After 10 years: €10,511 in the account, but only €8,211 in purchasing power
- After 30 years: €11,614 in the account, but only €5,537 in purchasing power

The number went up but the value went down

---

## Slide 7

>> **So how do I know if I'm losing money?**

The real return is what actually matters:

Real Return = Nominal Return - Inflation

At 2.5% inflation:
- Cash under mattress: 0% - 2.5% = -2.5%
- Savings account: 1% - 2.5% = -1.5%
- High-yield savings: 3% - 2.5% = +0.5%
- Stock market (avg): 8% - 2.5% = +5.5%

---

## Slide 8

A negative real return means you're getting poorer even as your balance grows

This is why understanding purchasing power changes everything

---

## Slide 9

>> **Can inflation be good?**

Inflation actually helps borrowers

If you owe a fixed amount and inflation rises, you're repaying with money that's worth less

Your salary may increase with inflation, but your debt doesn't

---

## Slide 10

>> **Inflation hurts, in general**

Even your emergency fund loses purchasing power

A fund covering 6 months of expenses today might only cover 4 months in ten years

Revisit it periodically

---

## Slide 11

>> **Bottom line**

What you can do:

1. Think in real terms. Always subtract inflation
2. Don't let cash sit idle beyond your emergency fund
3. Invest for growth: stocks have historically returned 7-8% after inflation
4. Revisit your plan regularly
