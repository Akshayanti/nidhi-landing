## Slide 2

>> **Cash Flow 101**

Cash Flow = Income - Expenses

- Positive: you earn more than you spend. Wealth starts here
- Negative: you spend more than you earn. Debt starts here
- Zero: treading water

---

## Slide 3

Ask someone how much they earn and they can tell you

Ask how much they spend and it gets fuzzy

Income is one or two sources
Expenses are scattered across dozens of categories and accounts

---

## Slide 4

Without visibility, small leaks become big problems

- The gym membership you forgot about.
- The streaming service you don't use
- The "occasional" dinners out

It all adds up

---

## Slide 5

>> **Kind of expenses**

- Fixed expenses:
  - Rent, mortgage, insurance, loan payments, subscriptions
  - Predictable. Hard to change quickly

- Variable expenses:
  - Groceries, dining out, clothing, entertainment, travel
  - Unpredictable
  - This is where you have the most control

---

## Slide 6

>> **Tracking Cash Flow**

A simple way to find your cash flow:

1. List your monthly income
2. List your fixed expenses
3. Track variable spending for one month (use your bank statement)
4. Subtract

That's your number

---

## Slide 7

>> **Example Cash Flow**

- ~50% on needs (housing, food, transport, insurance)
- ~20% for savings, debt repayment, or investments
- ~30% on wants

Rough targets, not rules

---

## Slide 8

>> **Savings Rate**

Your savings rate = (Income - Expenses) x 100 / Income

Earn €3,000, spend €2,400 = 20% savings rate

That single number tells you more about your trajectory than your salary

---

## Slide 9

If your needs eat 80% of your income, there's very little room to build anything

The 20% savings target is where wealth actually comes from

---

## Slide 10

Cash flow and net worth are linked

- Positive cash flow each month = your assets grow or your liabilities shrink
- Negative cash flow = the opposite

That surplus fuels everything: emergency fund first, then debt payoff, then investing

Track one, and you're managing the other
