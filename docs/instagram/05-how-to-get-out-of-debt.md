---

## Slide 2

Before choosing a method:

1. List every debt (balance, rate, minimum payment)
2. Commit to minimum payments on everything
3. Find your extra -- even €50/month helps

That extra is your weapon.
Both methods just aim it differently.

---

## Slide 3

The Snowball Method

Order: smallest balance first.

Pay minimums on everything.
Put all extra money toward the smallest debt.

When it's gone, roll that payment into the next one.

---

## Slide 4

Snowball example:

Store card: €1,200 at 12%
Personal loan: €4,000 at 9%
Credit card: €6,500 at 22%
Student loan: €14,000 at 4%

With €300 extra/month, the store card is gone in ~4 months. Quick win.

---

## Slide 5

Why snowball works:

Quick wins build momentum. Seeing a debt disappear entirely keeps you motivated to tackle the next one.

Trade-off: You might pay more total interest.

---

## Slide 6

The Avalanche Method

Order: highest interest rate first.

Pay minimums on everything.
Put all extra money toward the highest-rate debt.

---

## Slide 7

Avalanche with the same debts:

Attacks the credit card first (22%).
Takes ~17 months to clear it.
But every euro is fighting your most expensive debt.

Then: store card (12%), personal loan (9%), student loan (4%).

---

## Slide 8

Why avalanche works:

Mathematically optimal. You pay the least total interest.

Trade-off: Takes longer to see a debt fully eliminated. Can feel slow.

---

## Slide 9

Which one should you use?

Motivated by quick wins? -> Snowball
Disciplined and want to save the most? -> Avalanche
Want both? -> Start snowball, switch to avalanche after 1-2 wins

There's no rule that says you can't mix.

---

## Slide 10

Common mistakes:

- Skipping minimums on other debts (triggers fees)
- Taking a consolidation loan without fixing spending habits
- Stopping after the first debt is gone instead of rolling the payment forward
- Waiting for the "right time" to start
