# Appreciation vs. Depreciation: Why Some Assets Grow and Others Shrink

## Caption

Not all assets are created equal. Some grow over time. Others lose value the moment you buy them. The force behind both? Compound interest.

This is the most important concept in personal finance. Swipe through to see how it works, for you and against you.

## Slide 2

>> **What is appreciation?**

Appreciation is gaining value over time

- A home bought for €200,000 now worth €280,000
- An index fund that grew from €5,000 to €12,000

Historically:

- Stocks: ~7-8% per year after inflation
- Real estate: ~3-4% per year

---

## Slide 3

>> **What is depreciation?**

Depreciation is losing value over time

- A new car loses 15-20% in the first year
- After 5 years, it's worth ~40% of what you paid
- Cars, electronics, furniture, clothing - 
almost everything you buy for personal use depreciates.

---

## Slide 4

This isn't a moral judgment

Cars get you to work
Laptops help you earn money

But they are not investments
They're expenses that happen to be physical objects

---

## Slide 5

>> **Compound interest**

It is the force behind both appreciation and depreciation

- Simple interest: you earn on your original amount
- Compound interest: you earn on your original amount PLUS all the interest you've already earned

Your interest earns interest

---

## Slide 6

Same starting amount. Same rate. Massive difference

€1,000 at 7% for 30 years:

Simple interest: €3,100
Compound interest: €7,612

More than DOUBLE, just from interest earning interest

---

## Slide 7


Three levers of compound interest:

1. **Rate of return**: Higher returns = faster growth
2. **Amount invested**: More money compounding = more growth
3. **Time**: The most powerful lever of all

---

## Slide 8

>> **Compound interest on debt**

Credit card at 22%: interest gets added to what you owe. Next month, you're paying interest on the interest

€3,000 with minimum payments can cost you €6,000+ total

---

## Slide 9

>> **Why starting early matters**

Person A: invests €200/month age 25-35, then stops
Total invested: €24,000

Person B: invests €200/month age 35-65
Total invested: €72,000

At 65: 
- Person A has ~€281,000
- Person B has ~€244,000

---

## Slide 10

Person A invested 1/3 the money
and ended up with MORE

Ten extra years of compounding
beat thirty years of triple contributions

Time is the lever you can't get back

## Slide 11

>> **What appreciates and what depreciates?**

Likely to appreciate:
- Index funds
- Real estate (growing areas)
- Retirement accounts

Likely to depreciate:
- New cars
- Electronics
- Furniture

---

## Slide 12

>> **What you can do?**

1. Put money into appreciating assets
2. Minimize depreciating purchases
3. Eliminate high-interest debt fast
4. Start now. Time is the most powerful lever
