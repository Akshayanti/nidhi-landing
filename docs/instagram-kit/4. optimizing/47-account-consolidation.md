## Brief

person: Jiri, 38
situation: Ten years of jobs and offers left Jiri with 13 accounts at 7 institutions. Among them: a €30,000 pension from a job he left, with 0.75% fund fees where his current plan charges 0.05%; €6,000 in two old savings accounts paying almost nothing; and three dormant accounts charging €5 a month each.
withheld: How closing a card affects a credit score, every account type to list in an inventory, how in kind transfers move investments without selling, the transfer route by country, and when an old account is worth keeping.

## Day 1 (angle)

angle: Forgotten accounts are not free; they leak money every year
number: About €570 a year for Jiri: €180 in dormant fees, €210 in excess pension fees, €180 of interest his idle cash is not earning
takeaway: List everything, give each account a job, close or move the rest.
tool: The four step clean up. Inventory, categorise, migrate, systematise.

## Day 2 (angle)

angle: An old pension must move provider to provider, never through your own account
number: Paid out to Jiri, his €30,000 old pension could be counted as income: at an illustrative 30%, €9,000 in tax, with a penalty on top in some countries. A direct transfer moves all €30,000 as pension money.
takeaway: Ask the new provider to request a direct transfer; stop if any form offers to pay the money to you.
tool: The transfer check. Who starts it: your new provider. Where it goes: provider to provider. A form offers a payout: stop.

There are two ways to move an old pension. One of them can cost you thousands.

Jiri wants to move a €30,000 pension from a job he left. A direct transfer, provider to provider, keeps it pension money. If it is paid out to him first, many systems count it as income: at an illustrative 30%, that is €9,000 in tax, and some countries add a penalty on top.
