## Brief

person: Eva, 36
situation: One of Eva's investments is worth less than she paid, and another pays her interest every year in an ordinary account.
withheld: How a recorded loss offsets a gain, why the tax is delayed and not removed, the rules on buying back by country, when selling at a loss tends to pay and when it does not, the worked example of placing a €400,000 household's investments, and where these two techniques rank.

## Day 1 (angle)

angle: You paid €1,000 and it is now worth €800: does that fall lower your tax?
breadth: broad | Anyone whose investment has fallen in value
number: Paid €1,000, worth €800, a €200 fall that becomes a recorded loss only when sold. The reel says all three (owner, Nov 21: the first version was "very confusing"; council redesign)
takeaway: A fall in value is not a loss for tax until the investment is sold; where rules allow, the recorded loss can reduce the profit taxed on another sale.
