## Brief

person: Eva, 36
situation: Eva sold one investment at a €10,000 gain this year, and two others sit below what she paid. She also holds the same mix in every account.
withheld: The rules on buying back by country, when selling at a loss tends to pay and when it does not, which lot to sell, the full worked example of placing a €400,000 household's investments, and the order many planners describe: the mix, fees, tax advantaged accounts, then these two.

## Day 1 (angle)

angle: You owe tax on a gain and another investment is down: a loss can delay the tax, and it is a delay, not a saving
breadth: narrow | Needs a viewer who invests outside a pension and has sold at a gain
number: €10,000 gain taxed at 25% is €2,500; left invested at 7% a year for 20 years, about €9,700. The reel says €2,500, 7%, 20 and €9,700
takeaway: Selling at a loss can cancel a gain, so the tax is not paid now and stays invested; the replacement was bought cheaper, so a later sale can owe more.
tool: The delay check. What a later sale may owe, the rule on buying the same thing back, and whether the loss is big enough to matter.
send_to: Someone who invests outside a pension.
send_why: number

## Day 2 (angle)

angle: The same investments, held in different accounts: could that change your yearly tax?
breadth: narrow | Needs a viewer with more than one kind of investment account
number: €400,000 household: placing the income payers in a sheltered account removes €720 of yearly tax, the shares that take their place add about €180 back, about €540 less a year. The reel says €720, €180 and €540
takeaway: Investments that pay taxed income every year are commonly held where that income is sheltered; the overall mix stays the same.
