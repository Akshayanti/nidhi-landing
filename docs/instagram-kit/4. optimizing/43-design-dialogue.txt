## D. Script and structural problems

1. **The strongest fact arrives too late.**  
   The million euro gap should be the hook, not the fifth beat.

2. **Eva is over-explained.**  
   Her name humanizes the example, but she does not need a complete narrative arc. She is the input set, not the protagonist of an 88 second story.

3. **“Because she read it somewhere” is condescending.**  
   Replace it with “the calculator asked for a growth rate, so she entered 8%.” That makes the product pattern the problem, not the person.

4. **“Long run history supports all three” is too broad.**  
   A defensible rate depends on portfolio, fees, tax, currency, inflation methodology, and time period. Say they are “illustrative assumptions” unless the post substantiates more.

5. **“Plan on the low line” conflicts with the no-advice posture.**  
   Prefer “stress test the low line” or “check whether the plan still works in the low case.”

6. **The multiplier is currently presented as a complete projection.**  
   `monthly × 700/1,000/1,400` covers monthly contributions. Existing savings need their own `current balance × 3/6/10` estimate. The carousel contains both, but the reel narration does not make the distinction.

7. **The carousel’s slide 5 example should show its arithmetic.**  
   `€300 monthly plus €10,000 saved equals €240k, €360k or €520k` is correct, but opaque unless viewers understand that two different multiplier sets were combined.

8. **Slide 6 is a thematic detour.**  
   It is useful content, but it weakens this post’s clean story: one calculator, one assumption, three outcomes, one tool. If saves are the priority, I would replace slide 6 with a full worked multiplier example and move the holiday versus rent comparison to a separate post.

9. **Eighty-eight seconds is not justified.**  
   This concept has one reveal and one tool. About 46 seconds is enough to create tension, prove the claim, deliver the saved utility, and close. Longer runtime currently comes from restating the same conclusion in different card layouts.

The key design principle is continuity: the viewer should always know they are looking at the same plan while one assumption changes. The current reel repeatedly resets the visual context, which is why it feels professional but inert.
