## Brief

person: A couple and a single earner, anonymous
situation: Both follow the same rule: six months of spending saved as an emergency fund. The couple each earn €2,500 after tax and spend €3,600 a month. The single earner takes home €3,000 and spends €2,400. One job is lost in each household.
withheld: Why one person needs about 70% of a couple's budget in retirement, no survivor pension in either direction, longevity carried alone, how long term care systems differ by country, and how some tax systems treat single people.

## Day 1 (angle)

angle: The six month emergency fund rule hides a second income: the same rule gives a couple nearly 20 months of runway and a single earner 6
number: Couple: €21,600 fund, €1,100 a month short, nearly 20 months. Single earner: €14,400 fund, €2,400 a month short, 6 months. More than three times the runway.
takeaway: On one income the shortfall is the whole of your spending, so the same fund lasts a much shorter time. How far to adjust depends on how stable the income is and how fast it could be replaced.
tool: The one income check. Your shortfall is all of your spending; how stable is the income; how long would it take to replace it?

## Day 2 (carousel)

### Caption

Couples get a backup person by default. Living alone, nobody is named unless you write it down.

If one partner is in hospital, the other deals with the bank, the landlord and the doctors, often without paperwork. A person living alone has no such default. If nothing has been arranged, the law supplies one: a relative by blood, however distant, or a guardian appointed by a court. A few written choices replace that default.
