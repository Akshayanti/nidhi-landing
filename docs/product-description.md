## 5,000 character version

(Approximately 5,000 characters)

nidhi (nidhi.today) helps ordinary people understand their money, learn as they go, and plan their financial future. The name comes from the Sanskrit *निधि* (treasure, fund, wealth); in Vedic tradition, the nine Nidhis are the legendary treasures of Kubera, god of wealth. The tagline, "money, understood," is the whole pitch in three words: financial understanding should not be a privilege reserved for people who already know finance, can pay for advice, or have the time to become experts. nidhi is not an expense tracker, not a budgeting tool, and not an investment advisor. It is a place to learn how money works, apply it to your own numbers, and plan.

You can start knowing almost nothing. The learning path begins with what net worth is and why an emergency fund matters, and each idea connects to a free tool that applies it to your own situation. From there it goes on to investing, taxes, financial independence and the psychology of money decisions, and the planner (opening in stages) brings your whole picture together. nidhi keeps working as life gets more complex: loans and a home, a household, investments, retirement, and money in more than one country. Most consumer finance tools quietly assume one country and one currency; nidhi handles several from day one, for the people whose lives need it, without making anyone else feel it is not for them.

The planner is built around what you own, what you owe, what you earn, and what you expect to spend. From that picture it shows your real net worth, projects your path forward, models your retirement timing, and lets you compare major decisions before you make them. The questions it is designed to answer are the ones people actually ask: what is my net worth across currencies, when can I realistically retire, what happens to my plan if markets drop or my income changes, can I afford the apartment in two years, what is the impact of a future expense like tuition or a wedding, what does a career break do to my timeline. The assumptions behind each projection are surfaced, not hidden inside the engine.

Two free tools are already live on the site, with more on the way. The Net Worth Calculator, which works in one currency or several, accepts assets and liabilities in any of 29 currencies, converts everything to your chosen home currency using live European Central Bank rates, and shows how much of your net worth sits in currencies you do not actually spend in. The Loan Comparison Calculator compares borrowing offers side by side: APR, total cost of borrowing, refinancing scenarios, and per-vendor amortisation, without a spreadsheet. Both run entirely in the browser; the numbers never leave the page, and there is no signup.

Alongside the tools is a free personal finance blog organised as a structured learning path rather than a topical stream. It is published in stages and still growing, with more posts on the way. Current levels: discovery (net worth, assets, liabilities, debt, liquidity, emergency funds, cash flow, purchasing power, budgeting, credit, insurance), building (risk, asset classes, diversification, taxes, financial independence, passive income, loan terms, real estate, managing money across currencies, goals, financial dashboards), and psychology (loss aversion, mental accounting, present bias, overconfidence, framing, herd behaviour, narrative economics, money scripts). Posts cite primary sources under a "referential reading" section. The blog is free, forever, with no paywall and no "subscribe for the good stuff" tier.

What nidhi believes shapes the product. Financial planning should not require a financial planner. Tools should be usable without jargon or upsells. The site is built to be accessible to users mainstream tech often forgets, including users who are colour blind, use screen readers, or are otherwise wired differently. There are no ads, no data selling, and no behavioural retargeting. Searching "term insurance" on nidhi will not follow you around the internet for a week afterwards.

Privacy is treated as a first-class commitment, not a footer link. Free-tool calculations run entirely in your browser. The site is GDPR-aware, analytics live on European servers, and email addresses collected for early access are never shared or sold, with one-click unsubscribe from anything nidhi sends. The privacy policy is the source of truth, with a dated changelog so every material change to the site is on the public record.

The product is written and built by one person, under one name, working in public. Posts on the blog are practitioner-led: the writer builds the tools the posts describe, which means the writing is grounded in actually working through the numbers rather than in repeating talking points. Every article that makes a non-trivial claim points to its source under a "referential reading" section.

The full planner is opening in stages through an early-access list. In the meantime, the free tools and the blog are live, useful on their own, and free to use without an account. Made in the heart of Europe (in Prague), powered by word of mouth.

## 10,000 character version

(Approximately 10,000 characters)

### What nidhi is

nidhi (nidhi.today) helps ordinary people understand their money, learn as they go, and plan their financial future. The name comes from the Sanskrit *निधि* (treasure, fund, wealth); in Vedic tradition, the nine Nidhis are the legendary treasures of Kubera, the god of wealth. The tagline is "money, understood," and it is meant literally: financial understanding should not be a privilege reserved for people who already know finance, can afford professional advice, or have the time to become experts. nidhi combines a free, structured learning path, private in-browser tools that apply each idea to your own numbers, and a planner, opening in stages, that lets you create your own financial plan instead of going to a bank or a financial advisor. Equally important is what nidhi is not: not an expense tracker, not a budgeting tool, and not an investment advisor. nidhi does not tell you what to buy or which fund to pick. It answers the planning questions you would otherwise pay an advisor to answer, and it leaves the strategy to you.

The journey is learn, understand, apply, model, decide. Someone learns what net worth is, then works out their own. They learn why an emergency fund matters, then check where theirs stands. They learn about investing and financial independence, then model what steady investing could mean for their own future. Each step is useful on its own, and nobody has to finish the curriculum before the tools are useful.

nidhi also keeps working as finances become more sophisticated. Mainstream consumer finance tools quietly assume one country, one currency, one tax regime, and one retirement destination. Anyone whose real life does not look like that, expats, cross-border workers, freelancers with international clients, people planning to retire somewhere other than where they currently work, ends up rebuilding spreadsheets from scratch every time they want to answer a basic question. nidhi handles multiple currencies from day one rather than as an extension pack bolted on later. It is a differentiator, not a requirement: everything works with a single currency.

### Who it is for

Two situations bring people to nidhi, and they can overlap. The first is a confidence and knowledge problem: "I know I should understand my finances better, but I don't know where to start." That might be someone with a first salary, savings but no plan, or a feeling that financial terms are intimidating. They may have an entirely ordinary, single-currency financial life, and they are core nidhi users. The second is a complexity problem: "My finances have become complicated and normal tools don't represent my life properly." If "can I afford this?" cannot be answered without rebuilding a spreadsheet, nidhi is built for you. If your retirement plan changes depending on where you eventually live, nidhi is built for you. If you are comparing a property purchase, a career change, or another major financial decision and want to see the path each option puts you on, nidhi is built for you. If your financial picture is spread across accounts, assets, debts, and future expenses, in more than one currency, nidhi is built for you.

The product is not aimed at one demographic. It serves people building wealth for the first time, people deep into financial independence and early-retirement planning, people working out a traditional retirement with confidence intervals, people saving for a specific goal like a down payment, and people who simply want clarity about whether they are building wealth or standing still. The same engine answers all of those questions; only the messaging changes.

### How it works

nidhi is built around four pieces of your life: what you own, what you owe, what you earn, and what you expect to spend, each in whichever currency it actually lives in. From that picture, the planner does four things.

It shows your real net worth. Assets and debts denominated in different currencies are converted into the currency you actually spend in, and the underlying currency exposure is surfaced so you can see how much of your net worth depends on FX rates moving the right way.

It projects your path forward. Net worth, cash flow, and retirement timing are modelled over the long range, with the assumptions behind each projection visible rather than hidden. You can see how the path changes when savings, costs, returns, or inflation change.

It lets you compare decisions before you make them. Buying property, saving more, taking a lower-paying job, retiring earlier, relocating, a future expense like tuition or a wedding, an inheritance, a market drop, a currency move. Each scenario shows the impact on your net worth, your retirement timing, and your cash flow, side by side with your baseline.

It treats the user as the strategist. nidhi shows you the math and surfaces public-guideline reference points where they exist (the 28/36 percent debt-to-income bands, the three-to-six-month emergency-fund range, common safe-withdrawal-rate conventions). It does not tell you which fund to buy, which security is "high risk," or what your personal target allocation "should" be. You bring the strategy; nidhi is the calculator.

### What is live today

Two free tools are live on nidhi.today, with more on the way.

The Net Worth Calculator, which works in one currency or several, accepts assets and liabilities in any of 29 supported currencies, converts everything to your chosen home currency using live European Central Bank rates, and surfaces a currency-exposure breakdown so you can see how much of your net worth is denominated in currencies you do not actually spend in. It is a personal FX risk analyser, not just a converter.

The Loan Comparison Calculator compares borrowing offers side by side: APR, total cost of borrowing including fees, refinancing scenarios, and per-vendor amortisation schedules. The use case is the moment you are staring at two or three offers and want to know which one is genuinely cheaper once the structure is normalised.

Both tools run entirely client-side. There is no signup, no upload, no account, and the numbers you enter never leave the browser tab. URLs encode state compactly, so you can save or share a scenario without anything being persisted on a server. The full planner is opening in stages through an early-access list; the free tools are usable today.

### The blog

Alongside the tools is a structured personal-finance blog written by the founder under a single name. It is built as a learning path organised by level rather than as a topical stream, and it is published in stages, still growing, with more posts on the way.

Discovery covers what net worth is, how to calculate it, assets, liabilities, getting out of debt, appreciation versus depreciation, liquidity, emergency funds, income versus wealth, cash flow, purchasing power, why a euro buys more in some countries, saving versus investing, budgeting, credit, and insurance basics.

Building covers understanding risk, asset classes, satellite assets like commodities and crypto, diversification, getting started investing, taxes and tax-advantaged accounts, rebalancing, an introduction to financial independence, passive income, loan terms, real estate as investment, managing money across currencies, setting goals, financial-health metrics, and the idea of a financial dashboard.

Psychology covers loss aversion and the disposition effect, mental accounting, present bias, overconfidence and the planning fallacy, framing and anchoring, herd behaviour and FOMO, narrative economics, money scripts, and building an anti-bias financial life.

Two further levels, optimising and mastery, are on the roadmap. Posts cite primary sources under a "referential reading" section. The blog is free, forever, with no paywall and no "subscribe for the good stuff" tier. If something on the blog helps someone make a better decision, even if they never use the planner, that is the whole point.

### What nidhi believes

The values that shape nidhi are stated openly on the site.

It started out of frustration. The founder could not find a planner that handled an ordinary modern life with money in more than one currency, debts in another, and a future that crossed borders, and built it.

Financial planning should not require a financial planner. Whether you just got your first job or you are five years from retirement, the tools should be usable without jargon and without upsells.

It is built for people that mainstream tech often forgets. Accessibility is not retrofitted: the product is designed to work for users who are colour blind, use screen readers, or are otherwise wired differently.

The blog is free, forever. No paywalls and no "subscribe for the good stuff" gate.

No ads, no data selling, no behavioural retargeting. Searching "term insurance" on nidhi will not follow you around the internet for a week afterwards.

Made in the heart of Europe (in Prague), powered by word of mouth. The growth strategy is people telling people that nidhi was useful.

### Privacy and trust

Privacy is treated as a first-class commitment, not a footer link. Free-tool calculations run entirely in your browser, so the numbers you enter never leave the page. Analytics live on European servers. The currency-rate provider is backed by the European Central Bank and is the only third party the free tools talk to; it is documented in the privacy policy. Email addresses collected for early access are never shared or sold, with one-click unsubscribe from anything nidhi sends. The site is GDPR-aware as a baseline, and the privacy policy is the source of truth: every material change to the site ships with a dated changelog entry in the policy itself, which is unusual for a product this size and is a deliberate trust signal rather than a formality.

### Who writes nidhi

The blog and the planner are built by one person, working in public, under one name. The authority claim is practitioner and research-led rather than a wall of letters after a name. The writer builds the tools the posts describe, so the writing is grounded in actually working through the numbers rather than in repeating talking points. Every article that makes a non-trivial claim points to its source under a "referential reading" section, with the books, reference pages, and primary material listed explicitly. The editorial discipline is unusual for personal-finance content on the open web, and it is one of the project's deliberate differentiators.

### How to get started

The free tools are available now at nidhi.today/free, and the blog is at nidhi.today/blog. The full planner is opening in stages through an early-access list, linked from the homepage. There is an Instagram presence at @nidhi.today for short-form explainers, and an opt-in newsletter for people who want updates without checking back. nidhi does not run paid acquisition, affiliate schemes, or retargeting; the way most people find it is by someone else telling them it was useful.
