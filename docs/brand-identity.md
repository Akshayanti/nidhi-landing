# Brand identity: who nidhi is for, and how it speaks

**Last updated:** 2026-11-10

This is the reference for anyone writing for nidhi: the owner, volunteers, Claude, Codex and DeepSeek. Read it before writing copy, a lesson, a translation or a glossary entry. Colours, logo and tagline are in [`color-palette.md`](color-palette.md); Instagram has its own audience and rules ([`plans/PLAYBOOK.md`](plans/PLAYBOOK.md) §1, [`instagram-kit/BRAND-RULES.md`](instagram-kit/BRAND-RULES.md)).

## Who the site is for

The site is for a whole working life, from the first pay slip to retirement and after. Four readers, one site:

1. **Starting a first job, with no idea how to manage money.** Nobody taught them, and the words are intimidating. For this reader nidhi should be *the* reference they come back to: where to begin, what the terms mean, what to do with a first salary, and where they can learn more as they go.
2. **A few years into working.** The basics are in place and the questions get bigger: financial independence and early retirement (FIRE), saving for goals, planning for children, and working through what-ifs (a move, a career change, a market drop, buying a home).
3. **Close to retirement.** Turning savings into income, estate planning and its pitfalls, and the mistakes that are expensive to make late.
4. **Retired and settled.** Not here to learn the basics, but to judge them. This reader should find the site sound, see in it a fair summary of what a lifetime taught them, and want to send it to their children, nieces and nephews.

**The test for every page:** a first-jobber can follow it, and a retired reader would forward it. If a page is too simple for the fourth reader to trust, or too dense for the first to follow, it is not finished.

What follows from this:
- No single country, currency or age is the default reader. Examples rotate across countries and life stages (see `plans/blog-content-plan.md` Rule 7 for the currency defaults).
- Later-life topics (estates, pensions, care) are not niche: they are part of the site, and are written so a younger reader sees why they matter early.
- The site does not talk down to the young or assume the old know everything.

The Instagram account targets a narrower audience (PLAYBOOK §1) to grow. That audience is a way into the site, not a definition of it.

## How nidhi speaks

### Settled

- **Teach, don't prescribe.** The concept, why people use it, the evidence with its limits, where it breaks, what depends on the reader's situation (`CLAUDE.md`, "Editorial Voice").
- **Plain words.** Every term is explained before it is used.
- **The founder speaks as "I"** where a person is speaking, and is never given a gender, in any language.
- **The reader is never given a gender**, in any language. Each glossary says how its language avoids it.
- **No em dashes or double dashes** anywhere on the site.

### Tone: a polite friend a few steps ahead

*Owner's choice of relationship and founder's voice; rules agreed by the council and the whole tone approved by the owner, 2026-11-10.*

nidhi talks like a polite friend a few steps ahead: someone who has done the reading, checked the numbers and lived some of it, and remembers not knowing. That friend is also a teacher. The authority comes from knowing the subject, explaining it clearly and being honest about its limits, not from rank, age or distance. The voice is warm and at eye level, never rude, careless or falsely intimate. The reader is a capable adult who may know more about their own life than nidhi does. A 22-year-old should feel welcome; a 70-year-old should find it sound enough to send to their family.

1. **Teach, and leave the decision with the reader.** The concept, why people use it, the evidence and its limits, where it breaks, what depends on circumstances. No "you should", no disguised steps for someone's money decision, no superlatives the evidence cannot carry.
2. **Explain before relying on knowledge.** Every term before it is used, the reason before the definition, worked examples. Never "basic", "obvious", "easy" or "just".
3. **Respect the whole working life.** Organise around situations, not ages. Never infer knowledge from age, or assume income, health, children or a conventional family.
4. **Conversational and precise.** Plain words, natural sentences, contractions in English. No slang, emoji, exclamation marks in teaching text, or pet names. Polite always: if a sentence would be wrong to say to a stranger's grandparent, rewrite it.
5. **Acknowledge difficulty without inventing feelings.** Once, conditionally ("this can feel overwhelming"). Never "I know how you feel", no guaranteed outcomes, no reassurance in place of explanation.
6. **Name hard subjects plainly and gently.** Die, will, debt, estate, family conflict. No blame, drama or euphemism.
7. **Claims are supportable, reassurance included.** Sources, dates and jurisdictions; what is known, what varies, what no one can predict. "You're not alone" and other claims about how many people share a situation need a source, or are reworded.
8. **Personal voice and humour, sparingly.** The founder's "I" now and then, where experience helps; the teaching stays about the reader. Humour is rare and gentle, about systems and complexity. Never in tools, debt, distress, illness, bereavement, estates, family conflict, fraud, losses, errors or warnings, and never at the reader, their age, family or knowledge. Nothing may depend on a joke, and a translator may drop one.

### How each language addresses the reader

**The principle:** use the form, natural in that language, that a knowledgeable, polite friend would use with an adult of any age: warm and respectful, never intimate and never stiff. Do not copy another language's level of formality. Where no single form does both, prefer the one an older reader would not find disrespectful, and carry the warmth through sentence style.
